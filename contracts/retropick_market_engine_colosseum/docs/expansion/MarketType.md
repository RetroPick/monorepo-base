The market categories that matter beyond crypto — ranked by strategic priority
Immediate priority — gold, oil, FX: These require only new Pyth oracle feed IDs in your template config. No code changes to market_engine. Gold (XAU) and oil (WTI/Brent) are commodity price derivatives — the single most established category in global financial regulation, with 170 years of legal precedent. USD/MYR and USD/IDR FX pairs are especially powerful because they create a local relevance story for the SEA user base. A Malaysian or Indonesian user who cares about their own currency has a genuine hedge use case, strengthening the financial derivative framing further.
High priority — equity indices: S&P 500 and Nasdaq daily close markets are natural progressions. They target users who already watch US markets and have strong retention mechanics around US market open/close. The legal framing is clean — an index is definitionally a financial instrument globally.
Strategic priority — macro data events: Fed rate decisions, CPI prints, and jobs reports are scheduled, machine-readable, oracle-verifiable events with massive global user interest. They require a categorical resolver (hike/hold/cut) rather than your current Direction/Threshold/RangeClose set, but the architecture extension is minimal. Crucially, the CFTC's amicus brief notes that over 3,000 self-certified event-based contracts already include cryptocurrency price levels, GDP releases, interest rate decisions, and precious metals price movements. This is your roadmap in a single sentence.
SEA differentiation play — local weather and FX: This is underexplored globally but extremely powerful for a Malaysia-based product. A daily contract on whether Jakarta rainfall exceeds 30mm, or whether the MYR closes above 4.50, would have zero competition and genuine utility for SEA-native users. It's also the clearest hedge narrative: local farmers, SMEs, and anyone with FX exposure have real economic reasons to participate. This is the argument that separates you from "crypto gambling" and puts you closer to an agricultural futures exchange in regulator conversations.
The three things to never touch
Sports, politics, and entertainment are irreversibly classified as gambling in every Asian jurisdiction that matters. Asian governments have taken conservative approaches across the board, with gambling illegal by default and narrow exceptions only for state-run operators. The prediction market landscape in Asia is likely to remain a prolonged cat-and-mouse game with regulators as long as platforms try to serve non-financial event markets. Your architecture would never fit naturally into sports or political markets anyway — they require human judgment resolution, which your spec explicitly excludes from V1. This isn't a constraint; it's a competitive framing advantage. You can truthfully say to any regulator: "We only resolve from oracle price feeds. There is no game, no contest, no athlete, no politician involved in our settlement process."
