# Market Types

RetroPick has 9 market types. Each one asks a different kind of question, settled by real data from Chainlink oracles. No one decides the outcome manually — the data does.

This page explains what each market type means, how it works, and when to use it.

---

## How every market works

Before diving into each type, here is the basic flow that every market on RetroPick follows:

1. A market opens with a defined question and settlement conditions
2. You deposit into the outcome you believe will happen
3. At a specific time, the contract reads from a Chainlink data feed
4. The result is calculated automatically and winners collect from the pool
5. No one can change the outcome after the market locks

The only thing that differs between market types is **what question is being asked** and **how the oracle data is used to settle it**.

---

## 1. Direction

**The question:** Will this asset go up or down?

The most straightforward market type. You pick a direction, the oracle takes two price readings, and the side that matches the movement wins.

### How it settles

At lock time, the contract takes a price snapshot. This becomes the reference point. When the epoch ends, the oracle reads the price again. If the final price is above the lock price, Up wins. If it is below, Down wins. If the price lands exactly on the lock price, the market voids and everyone receives their deposit back.

### Step by step

1. The market opens and a lock price is recorded. Example: ETH at $2,500.
2. You deposit into Up if you think ETH will be higher at close, or Down if you think it will be lower.
3. When the round ends, the oracle reads ETH price one more time.
4. Up wins if close price is above $2,500. Down wins if below.
5. Exact match voids the market and refunds all deposits.

### Example

> Market: Will ETH be higher or lower in 24 hours?
>
> Lock price: $2,500
>
> Close price: $2,680
>
> Result: Up wins

### Best used when

You have a clear directional view on a short time frame and want a simple instrument. No thresholds, no ranges, just a call on direction.

## 2. Threshold

**The question:** Will this data point land at or above a specific level?

Threshold markets work with a fixed line. You pick which side of that line the data will be on when the round closes. This works with any oracle class — not just prices. Staking yield, GDP numbers, tokenized asset values, macro data releases.

### How it settles

A threshold value is set when the market is created. At settlement time, the oracle reads the relevant data and compares it to that threshold. If the result is at or above the threshold, the At or Above side wins. If it falls below, the Below side wins.

### Step by step

1. The market opens with a threshold defined. Example: 4% for ETH staking APR.
2. You pick At or Above if you think the APR will be at or equal to or above 4%. Or Below if you think it will miss.
3. When the round ends, the Chainlink rate feed reads the current staking APR.
4. At or Above wins if APR is at 4.0% or higher. Below wins if APR is under 4.0%.

### Example

> Market: Will ETH staking APR be at or above 4% at month end?
>
> Threshold: 4.00%
>
> Oracle reading at close: 4.3%
>
> Result: At or Above wins

### Best used when

You have a view on a specific level rather than a direction. Especially useful for yield and macro markets where you are tracking a number against a target, not watching a price chart.

### Oracle types this market supports

- Chainlink price feeds
- Chainlink rate feeds (staking APR, realized volatility)
- Chainlink SmartData feeds (tokenized asset NAV, proof of reserve)
- Chainlink macro feeds (BEA GDP, PCE, CPI, Fed funds rate)
- TrustedReporter (custom scalar data)

## 3. Range Close

**The question:** Which price zone will it land in at close?

Instead of picking a side of a single line, you pick one bucket out of several. The market divides a price range into sections, and only the bucket that matches the final price wins.

### How it settles

The market defines a set of price buckets covering a range. At settlement, the oracle reads the closing price and determines which bucket it falls into. Everyone who deposited into that bucket splits the pool from all other buckets.

Only the close price matters. Where the asset traded during the round has no effect on the outcome.

### Step by step

1. The market opens with several price buckets defined. Example: below $70k / $70k-$80k / $80k-$90k / above $90k.
2. You deposit into whichever bucket you think BTC will land in when the round closes.
3. When the round ends, the oracle reads the BTC price.
4. The bucket containing that price wins the pool.

### Example

> Market: Where will BTC close this week?
>
> Buckets: Below $70k / $70k-$80k / $80k-$90k / Above $90k
>
> BTC close price: $78,200
>
> Result: $70k-$80k bucket wins

### Best used when

You think the price will stay in a zone rather than move strongly in one direction. Good for low-volatility periods or when you have a view on a range rather than a breakout.

## 4. Velocity

**The question:** How much will it move, regardless of direction?

Velocity markets are about magnitude, not direction. Up 5% and down 5% are the same outcome here. You are betting on how much the price moves from its starting point, not which way it goes.

### How it settles

At lock time, a starting price is recorded. At settlement, the percentage change from that starting price is calculated in absolute terms. The result is then matched against the defined movement buckets, and the correct bucket wins.

### Step by step

1. The market opens and a starting price is locked. Example: BTC at $78,000.
2. Movement buckets are defined. Example: 0-1% / 1-3% / 3-5% / 5%+.
3. You deposit into the bucket representing how much you think BTC will move.
4. At close, the oracle reads the price and calculates the percentage change from start.
5. The bucket matching that move wins. A 3% rise and a 3% drop both land in the 1-3% bucket.

### Example

> Market: How much will BTC move in the next 8 hours?
>
> Starting price: $78,000
>
> Close price: $80,340 (a 3% move)
>
> Result: 1-3% bucket wins

### Best used when

A major event is approaching where you expect volatility but are unsure of direction. Fed announcements, economic data releases, large liquidation zones. You can profit from the move without calling the direction.

## 5. Ladder

**The question:** Which price tier will it land in, where higher tiers pay more?

Ladder markets work like Range Close but with a twist: each bucket has a different payout weight. Buckets that fewer people are expected to pick carry higher multipliers. You are choosing your tier and your implied risk/reward at the same time.

### How it settles

At settlement, the oracle reads the price and identifies which tier it falls into. The winning tier collects from all other tiers, with each tier's payout scaled by its weight. A higher-weighted tier paying 4x distributes more of the pool to its winners than a 1x tier.

### Step by step

1. The market opens with price tiers and payout weights defined.
2. You deposit into the tier you think the price will land in.
3. When the round ends, the oracle reads the price and identifies the winning tier.
4. Winners in that tier collect from the losing pool, scaled by the tier's weight.

### Example

> Market: Where will ETH close by Friday?
>
> Tier 1: Below $2k — weight 4x
> Tier 2: $2k-$2.5k — weight 2x
> Tier 3: $2.5k-$3k — weight 1x
> Tier 4: Above $3k — weight 3x
>
> ETH closes at $2,320
>
> Result: Tier 2 ($2k-$2.5k) wins

### Best used when

You want your payout to reflect the difficulty of your prediction. If you pick an extreme tier that most people avoid, the higher weight means a bigger return if you are right. Contrarian bets are naturally rewarded.

## 6. Convergence

**The question:** Are two assets getting closer together or further apart?

Convergence markets track the relationship between two assets. You are not betting on either asset individually. You are betting on whether the spread between them will narrow or widen by the end of the round.

### How it settles

At lock time, the spread between two Chainlink price feeds is recorded. At settlement, the same two feeds are read and the new spread is calculated. If the spread narrowed, Converge wins. If it widened, Diverge wins. If the spread is nearly unchanged (within a tolerance defined by the market), it voids and deposits are returned.

### Step by step

1. The market opens with two assets selected. The lock-time spread is recorded.
2. You pick Converge if you think the two assets will be closer in relative value at close. Or Diverge if you think they will be further apart.
3. When the round ends, both oracles are read and the new spread is compared to the lock-time spread.
4. Converge wins if the gap narrowed. Diverge wins if the gap grew.

### Example

> Market: Will the BTC/ETH ratio change this week?
>
> Lock ratio: 31.2
>
> Close ratio: 29.8
>
> Spread narrowed
>
> Result: Converge wins

### Best used when

You have a view on relative performance between two assets. If you think ETH will outperform BTC this month, a Convergence market lets you express that view directly without managing two separate positions.

## 7. Composite

**The question:** Will multiple conditions all be true (or some of them) at settlement?

Composite markets combine several independent conditions into one bet. Each condition has its own Chainlink feed and threshold. You pick whether the combined logic will evaluate as true or false at settlement.

### How it settles

Up to four conditions are defined, each checked against its own oracle feed. The market has a logic rule that determines how those conditions combine. The three possible rules are:

- **And** — all conditions must be true for Yes to win
- **Or** — at least one condition must be true for Yes to win
- **Majority** — more than half of the conditions must be true for Yes to win

### Step by step

1. The market opens with 2 to 4 conditions defined, each with a threshold and a data source.
2. You pick Yes if you think the logic will evaluate as true at settlement, or No if you think it will fail.
3. When the round ends, each oracle feed is read independently.
4. The logic rule is applied to the results.
5. If the logic evaluates to true, Yes wins. If it fails, No wins.

### Example

> Market: Will both BTC and Gold hit their targets by month end?
>
> Condition 1: BTC at or above $80,000 (using Chainlink BTC/USD feed)
> Condition 2: Gold at or above $2,100 (using Chainlink XAU/USD feed)
> Logic: And
>
> At settlement: BTC = $82,100 (true), Gold = $2,095 (false)
>
> And logic fails because Condition 2 is false
>
> Result: No wins

### Best used when

You have a macro view that involves multiple things moving together — a risk-on scenario where several assets rise simultaneously, correlated yields moving in the same direction, or a set of economic conditions all being met.

## 8. Corridor

**The question:** Did the price stay inside a band for the entire round?

Corridor markets track the full range of price movement throughout the epoch, not just the close price. You are betting on whether the asset stayed within defined boundaries at all times, not just where it ended up.

### How it settles

An upper and lower boundary are set at market creation. Throughout the round, the high and low prices are tracked using OHLC (open-high-low-close) data from the TrustedReporter service, which reads Chainlink feed history. At settlement, if both the high and the low stayed within the boundaries, Inside wins. If the price touched or crossed either boundary at any point during the round, Outside wins.

### Step by step

1. The market opens with a corridor defined. Example: BTC between $75,000 and $82,000.
2. You pick Inside if you believe BTC will never touch or cross either boundary during the round. Or Outside if you think it will breach the channel at some point.
3. Throughout the round, the highest and lowest prices are tracked.
4. At settlement, those tracked values are compared against the corridor boundaries.
5. If the high was below $82,000 and the low was above $75,000, Inside wins. Any breach of either boundary means Outside wins.

### Example

> Market: Will BTC stay in the $75k-$82k corridor this week?
>
> Upper boundary: $82,000
> Lower boundary: $75,000
>
> Weekly high: $81,400
> Weekly low: $76,200
>
> Both stayed inside
>
> Result: Inside wins

### Best used when

You think the market will be range-bound during a specific period. Low-volatility windows after large moves, quiet macro periods, or consolidation phases are good candidates. This market pays you for being right about the range, not just the direction.

## 9. Cascade

**The question:** How many resistance or support levels did it break through?

Cascade markets define a series of levels stacked in one direction. You pick a tier representing how many of those levels the price will break through. More levels broken means a higher tier. Higher tiers pay more because fewer people bet on extreme outcomes.

### How it settles

A set of price levels is defined at market creation, stacked either upward (resistance levels) or downward (support levels). The highest price reached during the round (for upward markets) or the lowest price reached (for downward markets) is tracked using OHLC data. At settlement, the number of levels that were broken is counted and the matching tier wins.

### Step by step

1. The market opens with 3 to 5 levels and a direction defined. Example: BTC levels at $80k, $82k, $84k, $86k going upward.
2. You pick the tier matching how many levels you think BTC will break through.
3. Throughout the round, the highest price reached is tracked.
4. At settlement, the count of broken levels is determined by comparing the tracked high against each level.
5. The tier matching that count wins.

### Example

> Market: How many resistance levels will BTC break this month?
>
> Levels: $80,000 / $82,000 / $84,000 / $86,000 (upward)
> Tiers: 0 levels / 1 level ($80k) / 2 levels ($82k) / 3+ levels ($84k+)
>
> Monthly high: $83,400
>
> $80,000 broken (yes), $82,000 broken (yes), $84,000 not broken
>
> 2 levels broken
>
> Result: 2 levels tier wins

### Best used when

You expect a trending move and want tiered exposure to how far it goes. Instead of a binary directional bet, you get a structured range of outcomes where aggressive calls carry higher payouts.

## Summary

| Market type | What you are betting on | Data used |
|---|---|---|
| Direction | Up or down at close | Close price vs lock price |
| Threshold | Above or below a fixed level | Close reading vs threshold |
| Range Close | Which price zone at close | Close price vs bucket ranges |
| Velocity | How much it moves | Percentage change from start |
| Ladder | Which tier at close, weighted payouts | Close price vs tier ranges |
| Convergence | Two assets getting closer or further apart | Spread between two feeds |
| Composite | Multiple conditions combined by logic | Multiple feeds and thresholds |
| Corridor | Stayed inside a price band all round | Full high and low during epoch |
| Cascade | How many levels were broken | Full high or low during epoch |

---

## Settlement is always automatic

Every market type on this list settles the same way: the contract reads from one or more Chainlink oracle feeds at the defined time, applies the market's formula, and distributes. No one reviews the outcome. No one can intervene after a market locks.

If you want to verify how any settlement works, the resolver functions are open source and can be called independently before you deposit.
